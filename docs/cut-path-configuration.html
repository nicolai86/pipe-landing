02. UI Layout & STEP Cut Path Configuration

Workspace Architecture Overview

RotaryCAM's interface is organized into three vertical zones: the Left Sidebar (3D Assembly Overview, STEP file upload/fixtures, active machine profile, and plasma cut parameters), the Center CAD & Simulation Workspace (3D Part Preview, 2D Unwrapped Feature Map, and Interactive Feature Table), and the Right G-Code Post-Processor Terminal (live compiled G-code with one-click regeneration and export).

Center Workspace Part Inspection & 3D Simulation
Right Terminal Live XYZA G-Code Post-Processor
Step 01

Load the Saddle Joint (Zero-Bevel) CAD Fixture

You can load any single-tube or multi-body .step / .stp assembly by dragging it onto the CAD Geometry dropzone in the left sidebar. To explore RotaryCAM immediately without uploading a file, use the built-in Quick CAD Fixtures panel right below the dropzone.

  • Locate the Quick CAD Fixtures section in the left sidebar.
  • Click Saddle Joint (Zero-Bevel) (DUAL-CURVE) to load a two-tube T-joint assembly where a branch pipe (OD 27.0 mm × 3.0 mm wall) copes onto a main chord tube.
Step 02

Switch Active Parts via the Entire Model Overview or Solid Selector

When a STEP file contains multiple bodies, RotaryCAM automatically separates every solid, detects its cross-section profile (Profile_HSS_Round, Profile_HSS_Rect, etc.), and identifies its inner and outer wall surfaces.

  • Entire Model Overview (Top-Left 3D Viewport): Shows the full multi-body CAD assembly in 3D. The currently selected tube is highlighted in amber/orange (Part 2: solid_2), while unselected tubes render in translucent steel gray. Click directly on any tube in this 3D overview to switch to it immediately.
  • Active CAD Solid Selector Bar (Top of Center Panel): Displays the active part's detected dimensions (Part 2: solid_2 (OD 27.0mm • L=54.8mm)). You can also switch between solids using the dropdown menu or the ◀ / ▶ navigation arrows.
Step 03

Inspect the 3D Part Preview & Extracted Cutting Contours

Directly below the solid selector, the 3D Part Preview & Cutting Contours viewport renders the isolated tube along with its color-coded 3D plasma toolpaths:

  • ● Red (Lead-In): Pierce location and entry sweep from the scrap region onto the cut line.
  • ● Cyan (Cut Contour): Kerf-compensated 3D cutting trajectory around the tube perimeter.
  • ● Green (Lead-Out): Overburn / circular exit path at contour completion.
  • Use your mouse to orbit, pan, and zoom the 3D part, or click the quick camera buttons (Iso, Side, End, Top) in the top-right corner of the viewport.
Step 04

Select the ENDCUT Row in the Feature Table

Beneath the 3D preview (and the expandable 2D Unwrapped Stock Feature Map), the feature table lists every cutting feature detected on the active tube—categorized as STARTCUT (chuck-side end cut), ENDCUT (free-end saddle/miter cut), or CUTOUT (internal holes, slots, and Copes).

  • Click the ENDCUT row (7E99200A-EBB2-4D59-9B57-AB5D515E73D3 at X = 48.3 mm, A = 267.5°) to highlight that specific saddle contour in the 3D viewport above.
Step 05

Click ⚙ Config to Open the Per-Contour Configuration Drawer

While each row provides quick inline dropdowns for Lead-In Strategy, Lead-Out Strategy, and Zero-Bevel toggle, clicking ⚙ Config in the right-hand ACTION column opens the full four-column engineering drawer for that specific contour:

  • 1. Lead-In Configuration: Choose strategy (Rotational Arc, Tangent Arc, Linear), sweep length (mm), scrap clearance (mm), and optional 3D Ramp Lead-In (Flying Pierce) to eliminate stationary pierce blowback.
  • 2. Lead-Out Configuration: Configure overburn extension (mm) and optional Zero-Overburn Early Torch-Out (synchronized M63 torch-off before contour completion to prevent exit divots).
  • 3. Corner Loop & Zero-Bevel: Enable rollout corner loops or configure Zero-Bevel Dual-Curve Compensation intensity, fade angle, and weld gap.
  • 4. Tabs Configuration: Leave uncut micro-bridges spaced around the contour to keep heavy slugs or parts attached during cutting.
Step 06

Enable Zero-Bevel Compensation (80%), Save, and Open 3D Simulation

On a 4-axis rotary plasma cutter, the torch is fixed perpendicular to the tube centerline (no 5-axis tilting head). When cutting a thick-walled pipe saddle or miter, the outer surface curve and inner wall curve have different axial heights at the saddle flanks—causing the inner wall to ride high and leave an open gap at the crown if cut strictly along the outer CAD curve.

  • In the Corner Loop & Zero-Bevel card, check ✓ Apply Zero-Bevel Compensation and set Intensity (%) to 80% (with Cope / Fishmouth enabled).
  • Click Save on the ENDCUT row (which updates to Saved ✓ and immediately recompiles the toolpath).
  • Click the ⚡ 3D Cut Simulation tab in the top workspace bar to inspect the compensated 4-axis motion.
🔬 How Zero-Bevel Dual-Curve Compensation Works

When cutting angled saddles or copes with a straight vertical torch, the cut face bevels naturally through the tube wall. If you cut only along the outside line, the inside wall of the tube sticks out too far at the flanks—leaving ugly gaps at the crown that take heavy welding or hand grinding to fill. RotaryCAM solves this by reading both the outside and inside wall curves directly from your 3D solid model. At every degree of rotation, it pulls the cut path back to the inner wall where needed, blending smoothly around the tube so your saddle drops onto the mating pipe with zero gap and zero hand grinding.

🛠️ Fabricating Bent Tubing? Try 3D Bent Tube "Cut-and-Bend"

Need to build a roll cage, header, bumper, or truss with curved tubes without an expensive mandrel bender? Drop your bent round pipe or square/rectangular HSS 3D model straight into RotaryCAM.

RotaryCAM automatically detects bends along the 3D centerline and unfolds the curved tube onto straight mill stock with precision mitered pie-cut relief slots and an uncut living-hinge spine.

Dead-Straight HSS Cheek Cuts: For square and rectangular tubing, RotaryCAM cuts dead-straight cheek walls across flat tube faces with built-in inner-wall relief clearance. Thick steel walls won't pinch or bind early—simply fold the tube by hand to the exact design angle and weld the seam flush with zero grinder fitup.

Step 07

Run the Interactive 4-Axis 3D Cut Simulation

The 3D Machine Simulation view parses the exact G-code emitted in the right-hand terminal (PROGRAM: G-CODE) and animates the 4-axis kinematics in real time:

  • Click ▶ Play in the bottom playback bar to start the simulation (you can also scrub the timeline, step -1s / +1s, toggle ⚡ 10x Rapids, or adjust playback speed from 0.25x to 8x).
  • Watch the Live CNC DRO (XYZA) overlay in the top-left corner of the viewport track the exact linear (X, Y, Z in mm), rotary (A in degrees), and instantaneous feedrate values as the tube rotates beneath the plasma torch.
Step 08

Verify the Completed 4-Axis Toolpath & Export G-Code

When the simulation reaches 100% (FINISHED), the full 3D toolpath trace—including rapid approaches, rotational arc lead-ins, kerf- and zero-bevel-compensated cut contours, and circular lead-outs—is rendered around the tube cylinder.

  • Inspect the right-hand G-Code Post-Processor Terminal to review the generated header comments, stock setup, synchronous M63/M62 THC locks, and coordinated G1 X... Y... Z... A... F... blocks.
  • Click the download button in the G-code terminal header to save your ready-to-run .nc program.
