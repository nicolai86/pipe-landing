11. Understanding G94, G93 Inverse-Time & G20/G21 Feedrates

Why flat-sheet F numbers drop by 25.4× when switching from mm to inches, why rotary G93 F numbers stay 100% identical, how SimCNC 4D trajectory spoofing works, and how to verify G-code before striking an arc.

How Your CNC Controller Controls Torch Speed on Plate & Tubing

On a flat plate, feedrate math is simple: your torch moves in X and Y at one set speed, such as 2540 mm/min (100 IPM). Your controller divides travel distance by time.

Add a rotary chuck, and linear rules break down. Your linear axes (X, Y, Z) move in millimeters or inches while your chuck (A) turns in degrees. When a line reads G1 A90 F2540, what is 2540? Degrees per minute, surface speed on a 1" tube, or surface speed on a 6" pipe?

Controllers solve this in three ways: G94 Units-Per-Minute, G93 Inverse-Time (standard on 4-axis rotary across almost all controllers, including SimCNC 3.6+ beta), or 4D Euclidean blending (SimCNC < 3.6). Controllers like Mach3, Mach4, UCCNC, MASSO, and Centroid Acorn let you type a manual round-pipe radius to scale G94. That breaks on rectangular HSS, angle iron, or saddles where the surface radius changes constantly. Below is what changes when you flip Metric (G21) to Imperial (G20), why G93 rotary F values stay identical, and how to check your G-code before firing the torch.

💡 Quick Rule: Metric (G21) vs. Imperial (G20)

Flat-sheet G94 feeds divide by 25.4 (F2540.0 → F100.0) because F is distance per minute. 4-axis rotary G93 feeds stay identical (F1000.0 → F1000.0) because F is inverse time (1 / Δt), where the 25.4 factor cancels out.

Step 01

Why Torch Surface Speed Controls Cut Quality (and Why Rotary Axes Complicate It)

Plasma cutting depends on matching arc heat to torch travel speed. An arc over 20,000°C melts steel while the gas jet blows molten dross out the bottom of the kerf. Drift off your chart speed by even 15–20% and cut quality drops right away:

  • Cutting Too Slow: Heat soaks into the steel. The kerf widens, the top edge rounds over, and heavy low-speed dross welds to the bottom edge. On thin-wall pipe (≤ 1/8" or 3 mm), going too slow blows holes in the wall and spatters slag inside the opposite pipe wall.
  • Cutting Too Fast: The arc lags behind the nozzle tip. You get steep 10°–20° bevels, glassy high-speed dross, and uncut bottom webs.
⚠ The Rotary Mismatch: Millimeters vs. Degrees

Linear axes (X, Y, Z) move in mm or in, while your chuck (A) turns in degrees (°). How much steel passes under the nozzle depends on your tube radius (R = OD / 2):

Surface Distance (Δs) = R × ΔA_radians = (OD / 2) × (ΔA_deg × π / 180°)

Rotate a 1" OD tube (25.4 mm) by 90°, and 19.95 mm (0.785") of steel passes under the torch. Rotate a 4" OD pipe (101.6 mm) by 90°, and 79.80 mm (3.142") sweeps past, four times further. A fixed chuck RPM crawls on small tube and races on large pipe.

Square HSS and angled pipe saddles are even trickier. The distance from chuck center to torch tip changes on every millimeter of motion, shrinking at flat face centers and spiking at corner fillets. Your G-code must adjust axis speeds on every segment to hold constant surface speed.

Step 02

G94 Units-Per-Minute: Flat Sheet & 3-Axis Y-Wrapped Rotary

Standard 2D CNC plasma tables, mills, and routers operate in G94 Units-Per-Minute mode. In G94, the F word dictates linear cutting speed:

  • Metric Mode (G21): F means millimeters per minute (mm/min).
  • Imperial Mode (G20): F means inches per minute (IPM).
  • The Conversion: Because 1 inch is exactly 25.4 mm:
    IPM = (mm/min) ÷ 25.4  ⇔  mm/min = IPM × 25.4

⚙ The 3-Axis Rotary Wrap & Fixed-Calibration Chuck Scaling (Langmuir FireControl)

Langmuir CrossFire PRO / XR tables running FireControl (v1.6+ to v24.1.6) have no 4th rotary axis driver, so you swap one linear motor cable onto the rotary attachment. Your post prints a Y-WRAP or X-WRAP header banner to match. Since CrossFire PRO and XR use dual Y1/Y2 gantry motors, choose Tube Along Y when plugging the single-motor X-axis into your rotary (X-Wrap).

Round pipe circumference unrolls directly into linear travel: Y = π × OD × (A / 360°) (or X in X-Wrap). Normal G94 feedrates apply (F2540.0 mm/min in G21 becomes F100.0 IPM in G20), with two rotary calibration modes:

  • Surface-Drive Friction Rollers (No Recalibration Needed): When your pipe rides on driven wheels, 1 mm (or 1") of roller travel rolls 1 mm (or 1") of pipe surface on any OD.
  • Rotary Chucks with Fixed-Calibration Travel / Rev (L_rev): Geared chucks take a fixed linear travel command (L_rev) per turn. Enter Travel / Rev (L_rev) once in Machine Settings. Your G94 feed scales automatically to the cylindrical DRO for any pipe OD without editing GRBL steps/mm between jobs, while (PS<feed>) keeps your true surface speed.
  • FireControl G-Code Syntax: FireControl toggles THC with H1 / H0 and probes with a two-stage G38.2 / G38.4 IHS cycle. It separates 0.020" (0.508 mm) backlash from thin-sheet springback (auto-zeroed on rigid pipe and ≥ 3/16" plate). On 3D ramps, it enforces a 0.55 s minimum G4 P dwell after M3 while holding H0 until cut height, then shuts off with M5.

Note: 3-axis linear wrapping only works on constant-OD round pipe. Square HSS, angle iron, and C-channel require 4 independent axes (X, Y, Z, A).

Step 03

G93 Inverse-Time Rotary (1 / Minutes): Why G20 & G21 Have Identical F Values

For 4-axis rotary tubes (pipe saddles, square/rectangular HSS, angle iron, C-channel), almost every controller runs G93 Inverse-Time Feedrate Mode. That includes LinuxCNC QTPlasmaC, Mach4, MASSO G3, Mach3, Centroid Acorn, grblHAL, UCCNC, FlashCut, EdingCNC, PlanetCNC, ShopSabre WinCNC, and SimCNC 3.6+ (which added G93 beta support in September 2026).

💡 Why You Should Skip the Controller's Manual "Rotation Radius" DRO

Mach3, Mach4, UCCNC, MASSO G3, and Centroid Acorn let you type a manual workpiece radius into a DRO to scale G94 speeds on round pipe. That shortcut causes three common failures:

  • Forgotten DRO Updates: Switch from 2" tube to 4" pipe without updating the DRO, and your torch cuts at double speed.
  • Fails on Square HSS & Shapes: On rectangular tubing, angle iron, and channel, surface radius changes from face center to corner fillet. A fixed radius cannot hold constant surface speed.
  • Combined X+A Moves: Fishmouth copes and branch saddles mix linear X/Y travel with rotary A spin in shifting ratios.

With CAM-computed G93 Inverse-Time, every segment carries the exact surface speed from your 3D part. Leave the controller radius DRO alone across all tube sizes and profiles.

ArtSoft Mach3 (R3.043.062) Checklist: For G93 rotary jobs, leave Use Radius for Feedrate UNCHECKED and G04 Dwell in ms UNCHECKED. Set LookAhead = 200, turn CV Feedrate OFF, and use a licensed Mach3 install (Demo stops at 500 lines). If you run a G94 round-pipe file with Mach3's radius DRO, set Rotation Radius to +0.0001 (never 0). Your G-code restores G94 right after every M5 and coaxial scribe stroke, and drops sub-0.002 mm (0.0001 in) linear noise in G93.

What Does an Inverse-Time Feedrate (G93 F) Actually Mean?

In G93, the F word does not mean distance per minute. It specifies the inverse of the time in minutes required to complete the move:

F = 1 / Δt_minutes = 60 / Δt_seconds  ⇔  Δt_seconds = 60 / F
  • If a G-code line has F1000.0, the move completes in 60 / 1000 = 0.060 seconds.
  • If a G-code line has F500.0, the move completes in 60 / 500 = 0.120 seconds.
  • If a G-code line has F2000.0, the move completes in 60 / 2000 = 0.030 seconds.

The Step-by-Step Proof: Why F_G20 Equals F_G21

How does CAM software calculate F in G93? It divides your desired plasma surface cut speed by the true physical length of the segment along the tube surface:

F = (Surface Cutting Speed) / (Surface Move Distance)

Let's walk through an actual rotary motion segment on 2" (50.8 mm OD, radius R = 25.4 mm) tubing using an exact 3-4-5 surface triangle:

  • Target Cut Speed: 2540.0 mm/min = 100.0 IPM (3/16" Mild Steel at 45A).
  • Move Segment: Linear ΔX = 1.524 mm (0.0600") combined with chuck rotation ΔA = 4.584°.
  • Surface Arc Distance: On 2" OD pipe, Δs_arc = 25.4 mm × (4.5837° × π / 180°) = 2.032 mm (0.0800").
  • True 2D Surface Hypotenuse: Δs = √(1.524² + 2.032²) = 2.540 mm (in inches: √(0.0600² + 0.0800²) = 0.1000").
METRIC CALCULATION (G21)

Speed = 2540.0 mm/min
Surface Distance = 2.540 mm
Δt = 2.540 / 2540.0 = 0.001 min (0.060s)
F = 1 / 0.001 min = 1000.0

IMPERIAL CALCULATION (G20)

Speed = 100.0 in/min (2540 ÷ 25.4)
Surface Distance = 0.1000 in (2.540 ÷ 25.4)
Δt = 0.1000 / 100.0 = 0.001 min (0.060s)
F = 1 / 0.001 min = 1000.0

🛑 Why Dividing a G93 F Word by 25.4 Destroys Your Pipe

Notice what happened: Both the speed (2540 / 25.4 = 100) and the distance (2.54 / 25.4 = 0.100) were divided by 25.4. The 25.4 unit factor cancels out completely!

If a CAM post-processor mistakenly divided a G93 F word by 25.4 when exporting Imperial G-code:

Wrong F = 1000.0 ÷ 25.4 = F39.37  →  Move Time Δt = 60 / 39.37 = 1.524 seconds!

The move takes 1.524 seconds instead of 0.060 seconds (25.4 times too slow). Your cutting speed drops from 100 IPM down to 3.94 IPM. The plasma arc stays stationary over the pipe wall, melts a massive blowout crater, ruins the nozzle shield, and destroys the workpiece.

The Hybrid Reality: G94 Probing vs. G93 Rotary Cutting

One 4-axis rotary program mixes both G94 and G93. Touch-off probing runs differently depending on your controller:

  • IHS Probing & Plunges Run in G94: Touch-off probing (G38.2 on grblHAL / bare LinuxCNC, G31 on Mach3 / Mach4 / UCCNC) and Z plunges move straight linear Z distance. They stay in G94 distance-per-minute: F1000.0 mm/min in G21, or F39.4 IPM (1000 ÷ 25.4) in G20.
  • Internal vs. Explicit Probing (LinuxCNC QtPlasmaC & MASSO G3): On 2D Flat Sheet (no #<tube_cut>, zero Z/A words), LinuxCNC QtPlasmaC runs probing, pierce height, pierce delay, and plunge inside M3 $0 S1 (ended by M5 $0) at F#<_hal[plasmac.cut-feed-rate]>. On Rotary & 3D Ramps (#<tube_cut>=1), QtPlasmaC yields Z to G-code: you get explicit G91/G38.2/G90/G1 Z#5063/G10 L20 P0 probes, M3 $0 S1 + G94 G1 Z plunges, and rotary lookahead (G94 on HSS flats switching to G93 on A moves). MASSO G3 + DTHC also probes and pierces inside M3 using its G200 cycle.
  • Contour Cutting Runs in G93: Once the arc fires and lateral/rotary motion starts, the code switches to G93. Each segment gets an inverse-time F word timed to the millisecond.

Step 04

SimCNC < 3.6 4D Trajectory Spoofing (SimCNC Rotary) vs. SimCNC 3.6+ (G93)

CSLab SimCNC (CSMIO/IP-M and IP-S controllers) uses hardware trajectory planning and sub-millisecond motion-synced digital pins (M62/M63). Rotary feed mode depends on your SimCNC version:

  • SimCNC < 3.6 (e.g. Stable 3.5): Lacks native G93 inverse-time support. In G94 mode, SimCNC calculates move length across all active axes as a 4D Euclidean vector:
    L_4D = √(ΔX² + ΔY² + ΔZ² + ΔA²)
    Here X, Y, Z are linear units (mm or in) and A is degrees (°). Your G-code outputs the spoofed F word on every line.
  • SimCNC 3.6+ (Native G93 Inverse-Time): SimCNC 3.6+ adds native G93 inverse-time support. Your program uses hardened G93 blocks with no 4D spoofing:
    • Identical Feeds & Blending: G93 F values match across G20 and G21 (4 decimals, 0.0001 floor). Blending uses G69 and metric G64 P0.3/P0.4 even in G20 (banning G61).
    • Micro-Moves & Arcs: Merges < 0.05 mm / < 0.02° segments and feathers 0.002 mm (0.0026 mm in G20) between pure-XYZ and pure-ABC moves. Formats 5-decimal G20 arcs (I/J/K within 0.005 mm, split full circles, no G90.1/G91.1).
    • Dwells, Parameters & Unwinds: Drops sub-1 ms dwells (G4 P≥1 ms). Stays under the #1999 parameter ceiling (with #4061 THC allowlist and G4 P1 sync). Keeps comments to 1 (...) per line and unwinds with phase-preserving G92 A (no G92.1, explicit G0/G1 reset after G80).

How Your SimCNC 4D Feedrate Is Spoofed (SimCNC < 3.6)

SimCNC < 3.6 does not know your physical tube radius. A standard linear F word makes rotary cuts crawl or race. Your post finds the true move time Δt from target surface speed, then solves for the exact F value that makes SimCNC's 4D planner finish on time:

F_spoofed = L_4D / Δt = √(ΔX² + ΔY² + ΔZ² + ΔA²) / Δt

What Happens in G21 vs. G20 Under SimCNC 4D?

  • Pure Rotary Chuck Spins (ΔX = ΔY = ΔZ = 0): When only the chuck turns, L_4D = |ΔA| degrees. In G94 / SimCNC < 3.6, F means degrees per minute:
    F_pure_A = (ΔA / Δt) = (Surface Speed / (π × OD)) × 360°
    Since chuck RPM for a set surface speed depends only on tube OD, pure-A moves keep the exact same F value in G21 and G20.
  • Combined X+A Moves (Fishmouths & Miters): In G20, ΔX is in inches (ΔX_mm ÷ 25.4) while ΔA stays in degrees. The 4D ratio shifts, so F_spoofed scales per line to hold your true chart IPM on the tube wall.

⚠️ The SimCNC M05 Deceleration Trap (Why M63 Px Prevents Divots)

In SimCNC, M05 calls a Python macro (m5.py). Standard M-codes are not motion-synced, so SimCNC slows all axes to zero before running m5.py.

Stopping with a live arc gouges a blowout divot in your cut edge. To prevent divots, your program fires a motion-synced pin (M63 Px) about 1.5–2.0 mm before the cut ends. The arc kills instantly at full cut speed while residual gas finishes the kerf, and M05 runs only after motion stops.

  • MASSO G3 + DTHC: Runs T112 M6 then G200 F... P... D... C... V.... Inside M3, MASSO handles touch-off probing, pierce delay, and plunge without manual G38.2 Z + G92 Z0 blocks. Manual probing applies only to legacy external-THC or custom dynamic-4D-Z setups. In Imperial G20, the F inside G200 still divides by 25.4.
  • LinuxCNC 2.9+ QtPlasmaC: On 2D Flat Sheet (no Z/A words, no #<tube_cut>), M3 $0 S1 handles IHS, pierce delay, and plunge internally (ended by M5 $0), so omit G38.2 and Z moves. On Rotary & 3D Ramps (#<tube_cut>=1 before motion), QtPlasmaC hands Z to G-code: you get explicit G91/G38.2/G90/G1 Z#5063/G10 L20 P0 probes and M3 $0 S1 + G94 G1 Z plunges, and rotary center-spotting (M3 $2) probes with G38.2 + G10 L20 and drops to standoff height before firing. (#<keep-z-motion>=1 stays reserved for non-M3 scribes and markers.)
  • ArtSoft Mach4 (Warp9 ESS / TMC3in1):
    • Modes & Wraps: Separates G90 and G91.1 (G00 G90 G17 G54 G40 G49 G80 then G91.1). Runs G94 for startup, rapids, and probes, switches to G93 for rotary strokes, resets mid-pack wraps via modulo-360 G92 A0.000 (no G92.1), and runs end-of-program unwind (G0 A<nearestRev>) inside the job frame before G92.1 + zero-motion G92 A0.000, then M30 + trailing % and newline.

    • Modes & IHS: Starts with G90 G90.2, M54 P0, and M55 P0 M56 P0 M57 P0 (canceling Transform, Warp, Swap). Opens in G94, switches to G93 only for rotary strokes, and restores G94 F... before safe-Z retracts and EOF.   • ShopSabre WinCNC (v3.1.06d+ via Help → About):
    • Units & Axis: WinCNC treats G21 as centimeters, so your post emits G22 for millimeters and G20 for inches (never G21). Match your SideKick rotary letter (A, W, or B) to WINCNC.INI.
    • Macros & THC: Emits M65 init, optional G9 S10, G94 before rapids and M62, L28.1 0-360 wraps, Pack-only L92 before M66 (keeping single-part G92 restore), and G4 X<sec> dwells.     • Subset & Datum: Assumes a pre-zeroed Z0 top datum. Uses core codes (G00/G01/G02/G03/G04/G17/G20-G21/G40/G90/G92/G94, M03/M05/M30), uppercase (COMMENT) lines (≤ 80 chars), and zero ;, %, G49, G92.1, or G10 L20.

📋 5 Shop Rules at the Machine Console
  • Rule 1 (Flat Sheet = Distance): On 2D plate and 3-axis Y-wrapped round pipe, G94 F divides by 25.4 in Imperial (2540 mm/min → 100 IPM).
  • Rule 2 (4-Axis Rotary = Inverse Time): On 4-axis rotary tubes, G93 F (1 / Δt) stays identical in Metric and Imperial (F1000.0). Never divide a G93 F word by 25.4.
  • Rule 3 (Probes & Plunges Scale): Touch-off probing (G38.2 on grblHAL, G31 on Mach3/UCCNC, G31.0-G31.3 on Mach4, G200 on MASSO) and Z plunges run in G94, so feeds divide by 25.4 in Imperial (1000 mm/min → 39.4 IPM). In LinuxCNC QtPlasmaC, 2D Flat Sheet runs IHS, pierce height, and plunge inside M3 $0 S1; Rotary & 3D Ramps (#<tube_cut>=1) emit explicit G38.2 probes and G94 G1 Z plunges.
  • Rule 4 (Pure A Rotations: G93 is Inverse Time, Not deg/min): On pure-A spins (ΔX = ΔY = ΔZ = 0), G93 F is 1 / Δt in min¹, never deg/min. Only G94 (like SimCNC < 3.6) writes pure-A moves in deg/min. At 5729.6°/min (2540 mm/min / 100 IPM) on 2" / 50.8 mm OD pipe, a 90° spin runs at F5729.6 in G94 / SimCNC < 3.6, or F63.66 in G93 (Δt = 0.01571 min = 0.942 s, 1 / Δt = 63.66 min¹). Both stay identical between G20 and G21.
  • Rule 5 (Keep Feed Override at 100% on Rotary Cuts): In G93, Feedrate Override (FRO) scales every block's time equally (50% FRO doubles cut duration). Leave FRO at 100% on rotary cuts: slowing down widens kerf and raises arc voltage so THC dives into the pipe, while going above 100% hits your A-axis speed ceiling on tight saddle crotches or square corners and gouges the edge.
