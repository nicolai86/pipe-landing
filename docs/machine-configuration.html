Set Real Motor Limits Before Your First Cut

A 4-axis rotary cutter moves X, Y, Z in millimeters while spinning your chuck (A) in degrees. When you roll around a square HSS corner or cut a steep saddle, your chuck must accelerate and decelerate fast while holding steady plasma surface speed. Entering your table's real motor limits keeps chuck drives from stalling on sharp corners and locks your Torch Height Control (THC) before the torch slows down.

Step 02

Set Axis Limits, Safe Z, Orientation & Polarity

The Axis Kinematics & Limits tab matches your CAM speeds to what your linear motors (X, Y, Z) and rotary chuck (A) can physically hold without faulting:

  • Rotary Drive Ratio & Direction: Enter your chuck gear ratio (such as 10:1 Rotary) and whether +A follows right-hand or inverted rotation.
  • Linear Axes (X / Y / Z) Limits: Set max rapid speed (mm/min), acceleration (mm/s²), and S-curve jerk (mm/s³) your drives can run without losing steps.
  • Rotary Chuck (A-Axis) Limits: Set max rotational speed (deg/min, e.g., 36000°/min = 100 RPM), angular acceleration (deg/s²), and angular jerk (deg/s³). Chuck rotation is almost always the bottleneck on square/rectangular corners, so these numbers keep your steppers or servos from stalling.
  • Safe Z Clearance Height: Sets the retract height (e.g., 25 mm) above your spinning tube's largest diagonal corner so rapid moves stay clear.
  • Chuck Jaw Dead Zone (65 mm), Min Chuck Grip (80 mm) & Steady-Rest Keep-Out Zones: Enter your jaw keep-out (chuckJawDeadZoneMm, default 65 mm), minimum clamp grip (minChuckGripLengthMm, default 80 mm), and steady-rest roller bands. Jaw collisions trigger a hard-block with actionable fix options (load longer stock, flip part, adjust gap, or override), steady-rest zones insert an automatic Safe-Z retract plus M0 pause, and both highlight flashing red in the 3D simulator.

NEW IN v0.7.1 Coordinate System, Axis Orientation & Motor Polarity

Every shop mounts chucks and loads plates differently. Set your rotary alignment, 4 travel modes, 6 flat-sheet origins, and motor DIR polarity once so your G-code cuts true without hand-editing coordinates or swapping stepper wires.

1. Rotary Tube Axis: Along X (Default) vs. Along Y (Gantry-Parallel)

Pick which linear axis runs down the length of your tube:

  • Tube Along X (Standard Rotary Table): Your chuck spins around the X-axis. Tube length runs along X, cross-torch travel moves on Y, and rotation spins A. Use this for standalone pipe cutters and front-mounted rotaries.
  • Tube Along Y (Gantry-Parallel / Outboard Rotary Trough): Use this for dual-purpose 4x8 or 5x10 water tables (such as FlashCut CNC or ShopSabre) with an outboard pipe trough along the Y rail. It applies a true right-handed +90° rotation (L → Y, T → -X). Length cuts move on Y, cross-torch moves (corner climbs or bevels) run on X, and speed/accel limits pull from the real moving axis. Because it is a true 3D rotation, asymmetric fishmouths, angled saddles, and hole offsets are never mirrored.
  • 3-Axis Linear Wrap (RotaryAxisMapping): On controllers without a 4th rotary axis (such as Langmuir FireControl or 3-axis Mach3), tube circumference wraps onto linear travel automatically: onto Y when the tube is along X (G92 Y0), or onto X when the tube is along Y (G92 X0).

3. Flat Sheet Orientation (0° vs. 90° CCW) & 6 Work Zero Origins

For 2D plate jobs from DXF or SVG files, match your table slats and pick where you zero the torch:

  • Sheet Orientation (0° vs. 90° CCW): Pick 0° (Part Length along +X) to run long parts along X, or 90° CCW (Part Length along +Y) to align them along Y. This rigid rotation (X' = -Y, Y' = X) keeps asymmetric brackets, offset holes, and scribed text never mirrored.
  • 6 Work Zero (X0, Y0) Origins:
    • Bottom-Left ([0, +X] x [0, +Y]): Standard 2D table default when standing at the front-left corner.
    • Top-Left ([0, +X] x [-Y, 0]): Zeroes at the rear-left corner with forward Y travel.
    • Bottom-Right ([-X, 0] x [0, +Y]): Zeroes at the front-right corner for right-hand loading.
    • Top-Right ([-X, 0] x [-Y, 0]): Zeroes at the rear-right corner with negative X and Y travel.
    • Center ([-X/2, +X/2] x [-Y/2, +Y/2]): Puts X0, Y0 dead center of the sheet. Great for small drops, round flanges, or remnants zeroed with a center punch.
    • Moving Sheet (-X → 0): For pinch-roller or plate-feeder machines pushing steel beneath a stationary bridge.

4. Hardware Motor Polarity (Invert X, Invert Y, Invert Rotary A)

💡 The Polarity Rule of Thumb

Set your Fixture & Datum (Tube Axis, Travel Mode, and Sheet Origin) first so the 3D view matches your table. Only check Invert X, Invert Y, or Invert Rotary A if a motor DIR wire is reversed or your chuck spins backward.

Enabling an axis invert performs the full geometric flip, not just a quick sign swap:

  • Negates that axis in every G0 rapid and G1 cut line.
  • Flips directional tool offsets, including G52 air-scribe and center-punch offsets.
  • Reverses arc directions (G2 CW ↔ G3 CCW) across G17, G18, G19 so lead-ins and kerf stay on the scrap side and never gouge your part.
✓ Multiple Profiles Saved Automatically

Keep separate profiles for different tables, chucks, or plasma cutters with + New or Clone in the left list. Every profile saves automatically across restarts.

⚠ Why G93 Inverse-Time Is Required for 4-Axis Rotary Cutting

Standard units-per-minute feedrates (G94 F3000) work fine on flat plate or round pipe. On square/rectangular HSS, angle iron, or pipe bevels, the distance from tube center to the torch changes every time the chuck turns. Controller "Rotary Radius Compensation" only handles one fixed circle and fails on rectangular corners, making your torch surge or crawl. With G93 Inverse-Time, every line carries F = 1 / Δt for its exact micro-segment duration (Δt), holding your cut-chart surface speed steady on any shape or corner radius.

Below the dialect picker, set your Post-Processor Compensation Strategy:

  • Enable Acceleration & Jerk Profiling: Models your real motor acceleration so feedrate ramps and THC freeze points match what happens on your table.
  • Smooth Rotary Corner Motion (Square & Rectangular Tube): Blends chuck rotation smoothly from flat faces onto corner radii to prevent jerks and motor stalls.
  • Enable Kerf Width Compensation & Kerf-True Tabs: Offsets inside holes (CW) and outside cuts (CCW) by half your kerf width so parts cut to size, and adds a full kerf width to micro-joint tab gaps so thin-wall tabs survive.
  • Rotary Small-Hole (60%) Bolt Speed Scaling: Slows enclosed bolt holes (≤ 25.4 mm / 1″) on round pipe and square/rectangular HSS to 60% speed, cutting out arc-lag taper so bolts drop right through.
  • Configurable ERW Weld-Seam Pierce Avoidance: Tell the software where your tube's internal ERW weld seam sits in the chuck on round pipe or square/rectangular HSS, and pierces automatically shift onto clean parent steel away from heavy internal weld flash.
  • Heat-Optimized vs. One-Direction Cut Ordering: Alternate holes and slots along the stick to prevent heat bow, or cut straight from the free end toward the chuck.
Step 04

Set Synchronous THC Lock & Plasma Hardware

Open THC & Plasma Hardware. Torch Height Control (THC) locking makes or breaks 4-axis tube cuts.

Around rectangular tube corners or steep saddle peaks, your rotary chuck slows down to stay inside its acceleration limit. Whenever cutting speed drops, the plasma arc widens and arc voltage spikes. If your voltage THC stays unlocked, it reads that spike as "torch too high" and drives your torch straight into the tube.

  • Plasma Torch / Cut Chart Family: Pick your torch family once per machine profile:
    • Hypertherm SYNC (3.2 mm / 0.125" cut height): Powermax 45/65/85/105 SYNC single-piece cartridge (810500MU Rev 4, 45A–105A).
    • Hypertherm Duramax / Legacy 30XP–105 (1.5 mm / 0.060" cut height): Powermax 30XP through 105 torches with multi-piece shielded consumables (30A–105A + 45A FineCut).
    • Generic Air Plasma (1.5 mm / 0.060" cut height): Blowback torches (30A, 45A, 60A, 80A, ~106–128 V, 72 PSI) for Razorweld 45, PrimeWeld Cut60, Everlast PowerPlasma, and Thermal Dynamics Cutmaster.
    Your standoff height (1.5 mm vs 3.2 mm), arc voltage, and pierce delay load automatically for every tube or flat sheet.
  • THC Mode & Lock Command Syntax: Pick how your G-code locks and unlocks THC, such as synced digital outputs (M63 P0 lock / M62 P0 unlock with zero lookahead pause) or controller-specific M-codes.
  • Dynamic Corner Lock Threshold (%): Locks Z height control whenever real surface speed drops below your threshold (such as 80% of cut speed), and unlocks once full speed returns.
  • Lead-In / Lead-Out THC Lock: Holds THC locked across pierce, ramp lead-in, and overburn lead-out while the arc settles or crosses an open kerf.
⚡ Why Synchronous M62/M63 Matters

Standard M64/M65 or custom M-codes flush the motion planner on many controllers, stalling the torch for a split second and blowing a divot in your tube. Synced M62/M63 commands flip the THC lock pin right on the next G1 move at full lookahead speed.

Step 05

Set Zero-Bevel & Corner Loop Defaults and Save

Open Zero-Bevel & Corner Loops to set default compensation for new parts (you can still override any individual cut later):

  • Corner Looping (Sharp Direction Changes): Set the corner angle range (90°–180°) and loop radius (3 mm) for scrap-side rollout loops that keep outside corners sharp.
  • Save & Switch Profiles: Use + New, Clone, and ★ Active in the bottom-left bar, then click green Save Profile in the top-right corner.

Step 07

Controller XML Import Warnings FAQ

When you import a controller XML file, the importer flags settings that can stall your rotary chuck, ruin pierce delays, or scale parts wrong. Look up your warning below to see what to fix on your table before cutting.

ArtSoft Mach3 • Profile .xml Warnings • Mach3 Page →

1. ArtSoft Mach3 (.xml) Import Warnings

Unrecognized or Missing <Units>

What it means: Your Mach3 XML did not specify inches or millimeters, so RotaryCAM defaulted Machine Units to mm. If your steps/unit in Motor Tuning are calibrated in inches, parts will cut 25.4x the wrong size.
What to do: Check Machine Units (mm vs. in) in the RotaryCAM Machines modal and match your Mach3 Motor Tuning units.

Motor 3 (A-Axis) Slaved to X / Y / Z (Slave0=0/1/2)

What it means: Your table uses Motor 3 (A-axis) as the second motor on a dual-drive gantry (common on Bulltear Plasma and HVAC gantry tables) instead of a rotary chuck.
What to do: Flat-sheet DXF/SVG cuts work right away with zero changes. To cut round pipe on a dual-drive table, either switch Rotary Axis Mapping to 3-Axis Y-Axis Wrap (and plug your rotary into Y), or unslave Motor 3 in Mach3 (Config → Slave Axis) if you move dual-drive slaving to hardware or the B-axis.

A-Axis Is Set to Linear Mode (<AAngular>=0)

What it means: Mach3 treats your A-axis as linear millimeters or inches instead of degrees, so G93 rotary speeds and chuck rotations will be way off.
What to do: In Mach3, open Config → General Config, check A-Axis is Angular, save, and re-import your XML (or type your true A-axis deg/min limits directly into RotaryCAM).

4th Axis Disabled (<AAxisOn>=0)

What it means: The A-axis output is turned off in your Mach3 profile.
What to do: Flat-sheet cuts work as-is with no changes. For 4-axis pipe cutting, enable Axis A in Mach3 (Config → Ports & Pins → Motor Outputs), or switch RotaryCAM to 3-Axis Y-Axis Wrap.

Spindle Spin-Up / Spin-Down Delay Active (SpinDelayCW > 0)

What it means: Mach3 adds an extra hidden pause after every M3 torch fire on top of RotaryCAM's G04 pierce delay, causing blown pierces and arc-loss faults.
What to do: Open Config → Ports & Pins → Spindle Setup in Mach3 and set CW and CCW Delay Spin UP and Spin DOWN to 0.

Mill / Spindle Screenset Detected

What it means: Your Mach3 profile loads a router or mill screenset (.set) instead of a plasma screenset, so on-screen THC controls and Arc OK LEDs may be missing.
What to do: Load a plasma screenset in Mach3 (View → Load Screens), or verify your M3/M5 torch output and THC pins before cutting.

G04 Dwell in Milliseconds (<DwellinMilli>=1)

What it means: Mach3 reads G04 P0.5 as 0.5 milliseconds instead of 0.5 seconds, so your torch plunges before piercing through the plate.
What to do: Open Config → General Config in Mach3 and uncheck G04 Dwell in ms so Mach3 reads RotaryCAM's G04 P dwells in seconds.

Plasma Mode or CV Feedrate Active (<PlasmaMode>=1 / <CVFeedOn>=1)

What it means: Mach3's built-in Plasma Mode, CV Dist Tolerance, and CV Feedrate fight G93 inverse-time rotary blending and cause choppy chuck motion.
What to do: In Mach3 Config → General Config, uncheck Plasma Mode and CV Dist Tolerance, then open the Settings (Alt-6) tab and turn CV Feedrate OFF.

LookAhead Too High (<LookAhead> > 200)

What it means: LookAhead values above 200 lines in Mach3 can overflow the motion planner buffer and stutter on dense 4-axis tube saddle toolpaths.
What to do: Open Config → General Config in Mach3 and set LookAhead to 200 lines.

Absolute IJ Arc Mode (<IJMode>=0)

What it means: Your Mach3 default is Absolute IJ instead of Incremental IJ. RotaryCAM emits G91.1 at the top of every file to force Incremental IJ, so normal runs cut fine, but mid-program restarts can drop back to Absolute IJ and throw crop-circle arcs.
What to do: Switch IJ Mode to Inc in Mach3 Config → General Config so mid-program Run From Here restarts stay safe.

Safe Z Height Zero or Negative (m_SafeZ <= 0)

What it means: Mach3 has no positive Safe Z retract height configured, so pausing or stopping mid-cut will not clear clamps or rotating tube corners.
What to do: Set a positive Safe Z height in Mach3 (Config → Safe_Z Setup) and verify your Safe Z Clearance in RotaryCAM clears your biggest tube diagonal.

Warp9 ESS + G28.1 Probing Warning

What it means: Selecting G28.1 touch-off probing with a Warp9 Ethernet SmoothStepper (ESS) often hangs the controller during floating-head probing.
What to do: Keep RotaryCAM's Touch-Off Strategy on G31 Probe (the Mach3 default) instead of G28.1.

FlashCut CNC • Setup.xml Warnings • FlashCut Page →

2. FlashCut CNC (Setup.xml) Import Warnings

Unrecognized or Missing SystemUnits

What it means: RotaryCAM could not read SystemUnits from your FlashCut Setup.xml and defaulted Machine Units to mm.
What to do: Check Machine Units (mm vs. in) in the RotaryCAM Machines modal and match your FlashCut table units.

Missing or Unexpected AxisParallel

What it means: Your Setup.xml did not specify which gantry rail your rotary attachment runs along, so RotaryCAM defaulted Rotary Tube Axis to X.
What to do: If your rotary chuck sits parallel to the Y gantry rail, select Parallel to Y-Axis in RotaryCAM's Machines modal.

Plasma Head ID Not Found in <Machine><FabHeads>

What it means: RotaryCAM could not match your requested Plasma Head ID or found no *PlasmaHead entries in your FlashCut XML, so head ID, arc transfer, and torch M-codes could not be auto-filled.
What to do: Pick your active plasma head from the dropdown in RotaryCAM, or type your Head ID (e.g. Hplasma1), Arc Transfer Input, and M50/M51 M-codes manually.

ZeroLiftAxisPositionAfterTouchOff="False"

What it means: FlashCut is not resetting its Z lift coordinate to zero after G605 touch-off probing. Standard 2D flat-sheet and round-pipe cuts work as-is, but 3D Ramp Lead-Ins and 4D square/angle tubes need a zeroed Z datum.
What to do: Enable Zero Lift Axis Position After Touch Off inside FlashCut (Setup → FabHeads) before running 3D ramps or 4D structural tubing.

Missing M101 I<n> Arc Transfer Input

What it means: RotaryCAM could not find M101 I<n> in your Cut2D start macro (or had to guess from InputLines), so it cannot confirm which input waits for Arc OK.
What to do: Open FlashCut (Setup → I/O → Inputs), find your Arc Transfer / Arc OK input line number, and enter that number in RotaryCAM's FlashCut settings.

CSLab SimCNC 3.6 • config.xml Warnings • SimCNC Page →

3. CSLab SimCNC 3.6 (config.xml) Import Warnings

Unrecognized or Missing <baseUnits>

What it means: Your SimCNC config.xml did not list <baseUnits>, so RotaryCAM defaulted Machine Units to mm.
What to do: Check Machine Units (mm vs. in) in RotaryCAM and confirm it matches your SimCNC Configuration units.

axis_3 (A-Axis) Disabled, Unbound, or Set to linear

What it means: In SimCNC, axis_3 (A) is turned off, has no MotionKit assigned, or is configured as a linear axis instead of angular.
What to do: Flat-sheet cuts work as-is. For 4-axis pipe cutting, open SimCNC (Configuration → Axes), enable axis_3, set its type to angular, bind a MotionKit, and re-import config.xml.

Missing <motionKit_N> or Mismatched Dual-Drive Gantry Limits

What it means: Either a <motionKit_N> block is missing under <device><modules>, or the two motors on a dual-drive gantry have different velocity, acceleration, or jerk limits (which can rack the gantry).
What to do: Copy Max Velocity, Accel, and Jerk from SimCNC (Configuration → Modules) into RotaryCAM manually, and make sure both dual-drive gantry MotionKits use identical limits in SimCNC.

<ijkMode> Not Set to relative

What it means: SimCNC is expecting Absolute IJK arc centers while RotaryCAM outputs Incremental (Relative) IJK arcs (and does not emit G90.1/G91.1 on SimCNC), causing huge unwanted arc loops or soft-limit errors.
What to do: Open SimCNC (Configuration → General) and set Arc IJK Mode to Relative.
