Why Accurate Machine Configuration Matters

Unlike flat-sheet 2D plasma tables, a 4-axis rotary tube cutter simultaneously coordinates linear axes (X, Y, Z) in millimeters with a rotary chuck axis (A) in degrees. When sweeping around rectangular HSS tube corners or cutting deep pipe saddles, the rotary chuck must rapidly accelerate and decelerate while keeping the plasma arc's surface velocity constant. Pipe CAM uses your machine profile's exact physical limits to compute S-curve trajectories, damp rotary corner singularities, and inject synchronous THC lock commands before the torch slows down.

Step 01

Open the Machine Profiles & Kinematics Configuration

From the main Pipe CAM workspace, locate the CNC Plasma Kinematics summary card at the bottom of the left sidebar. This card displays your currently active rotary ratio, rapid limits, Z clearance, and post-processor dialect at a glance.

  • Click the ⚙ Config button in the CNC Plasma Kinematics panel on the left sidebar, or
  • Click the ⚙ Machines button in the top navigation bar at any time to open the Machine Profiles & Kinematics modal.
Step 02

Configure Accurate Axis Kinematics, Velocity, Acceleration & Safe Z Limits

The first tab, Axis Kinematics & Limits, defines the physical capabilities of your CNC table's linear motors (X, Y, Z) and rotary chuck drive (A). Entering accurate numbers here is essential because Pipe CAM's internal trajectory planner simulates your machine's exact physics.

  • Rotary Drive Ratio & Direction: Specify your chuck reduction ratio (e.g., 10:1 Rotary) and whether +A rotation follows right-hand or inverted conventions.
  • Linear Axes (X / Y / Z) Limits: Set the true maximum rapid velocity (mm/min), maximum acceleration (mm/s²), and S-curve jerk (mm/s³) your drives can sustain without losing steps or faulting.
  • Rotary Chuck (A-Axis) Limits: Set the maximum rotational speed (deg/min, e.g., 36000°/min = 100 RPM), angular acceleration (deg/s²), and angular jerk (deg/s³). During rectangular tube corner transitions, the A-axis is almost always the physical bottleneck—Pipe CAM uses these limits to automatically throttle cutting feedrates around corners.
  • Safe Z Clearance Height: Defines the safe retract envelope (e.g., 25 mm) above the rotating tube's maximum diagonal radius so rapid moves never clip a spinning square/rectangular corner.
⚙ Configurable X-Axis Travel & Start Direction (Workpiece Moves vs. Torch Moves)

Plasma rotary tables move in one of two physical ways depending on whether your chuck pushes the pipe or your torch gantry rolls along the bed. Configure your machine's exact kinematic direction in the Axis Kinematics & Limits tab:

  • Workpiece Moves on X (SimCNC Rotary Chuck Default): For rotary tables where the chuck carriage pushes and pulls the tube along the bed beneath a stationary-X plasma torch. RotaryCAM zeroes at the far right-hand free end of the stock using G92 X-Length (e.g. G92 X-6000.00 Y0 Z0 A0). The machine cuts each finished tube from the free end first and feeds backward toward X0 at the chuck face, so finished parts drop cleanly onto the scrap table while the remaining tube stays rigidly chucked.
  • Torch Moves on X (0 to max(X)): For rotary attachments mounted inside a standard cutting table where the stock stays parked in the chuck and the torch gantry rolls down the tube. RotaryCAM sets your stock datum at G92 X0 Y0 Z0 A0 and cuts forward from X = 0 toward +max(X) along the bed.

Selecting the matching travel direction ensures your G-code programs never trip machine limit switches, cut in reverse, or drop stock out of the chuck prematurely.

✓ Multiple Profiles Persisted Across Restarts

You can maintain separate profiles for different machines, rotary chucks, or plasma power sources using the left list (+ New or Clone). All configured profiles are automatically saved and persisted across browser/app restarts.

Step 03

Select Controller Dialect (G94 vs. G93) & Post-Processor Compensation

Switch to the Controller & Compensation tab to select how feedrates and toolpath compensations are formatted in your output G-code.

  • SimCNC 3.5 IP-M/S (G94 + G93 4-Axis Emulation) — Recommended for CSLab SimCNC 3.5: Uses RotaryCAM's hybrid feedrate scaling for older SimCNC releases. Scales F words so coordinated XYZA moves cut at true surface speed even on firmware revisions where native G93 has rotary blending quirks.
  • SimCNC 3.6 Beta (G93 Inverse-Time Feed Mode) — Emits true ANSI/ISO G93 inverse-time feed words (F = 1 / Δt in inverse minutes) on every coordinated cutting segment, leveraging SimCNC 3.6's upgraded 4-axis trajectory planner for smooth rotary motion.
  • Standard Controller (Native G94 Units-Per-Minute) — Standard G94 feedrate mode for controllers (such as Mach4 or LinuxCNC) that compute rotary radius compensation internally or require standard units-per-minute F words.
⚠ Critical: Only Select Pure G93 If Your Controller Natively Supports It

In G93 Inverse-Time mode, the F word specifies how many times per minute a single micro-segment could be completed (1 / minutes), resulting in large numeric F values on short segments. Only select a pure G93 dialect if your CNC controller natively supports and properly blends G93 inverse-time motion. If your controller expects standard G94 feedrates (or is running SimCNC 3.5), select SimCNC 3.5 (G94 + G93 Emulation) or Standard Controller (Native G94) instead.

Beneath the dialect selector, configure your Post-Processor Compensation Strategy:

  • Enable CAM Velocity Profiling (S-Curve Acceleration & Jerk): Simulates forward/backward S-curve acceleration in software so velocity transitions and THC lock points match physical reality.
  • Enable ADLS Singularity Damping (HSS Corners): Applies an Adaptive Damped Least Squares filter to prevent A-axis angular velocity spikes as the toolpath transitions from a flat HSS wall onto a tight corner radius.
  • Enable Offline Kerf Compensation: Offsets internal holes (CW) and external contours (CCW) in 3D surface space by half your plasma kerf width so parts mate with zero grinding.
Step 04

Configure Synchronous THC Lock/Unlock & Plasma Hardware

Select the THC & Plasma Hardware tab. On a 4-axis tube cutter, Torch Height Control (THC) configuration is one of the most important settings in the entire CAM pipeline.

When the torch rolls around a rectangular tube corner or a steep saddle apex, the rotary chuck must slow down to stay within its angular acceleration limit. Whenever plasma cutting speed drops, the plasma arc column widens and arc voltage rises sharply. An unlocked voltage-based THC misinterprets that voltage spike as "torch too high" and drives the torch straight into the steel tube.

  • THC Mode & Lock Command Syntax: Choose how Pipe CAM emits THC freeze/unfreeze signals in your G-code—such as synchronous digital output macros (M63 P0 to lock / M62 P0 to unlock without motion planner pauses) or controller-specific M-codes.
  • Dynamic Corner Lock Threshold (%): Automatically locks Z-axis height regulation whenever actual surface velocity drops below the configured percentage (e.g., 80%) of target cutting speed, and unlocks it once full surface speed resumes.
  • Lead-In / Lead-Out THC Lock: Keeps THC locked during pierce, ramp lead-in, and overburn lead-out phases where the plasma arc is stabilizing or crossing an existing kerf.
⚡ Why Synchronous M62/M63 Matters

Standard M64/M65 or custom M-codes on many controllers force an exact-stop motion buffer flush, causing the torch to pause momentarily and blow a divot in the tube wall. Synchronous M62/M63 commands toggle the THC lock pin in lockstep with the next G1 motion block at full lookahead speed.

Step 05

Set Default Zero-Bevel & Corner Loop Parameters and Save Profile

Finally, open the Zero-Bevel & Corner Loops tab to define the default geometric compensation values applied to new contours (which can still be overridden per contour during part inspection):

  • Zero-Bevel Dual-Curve Compensation Defaults: Configure whether Zero-Bevel parameters are auto-estimated from saddle depth, along with the default Fade Angle (90°), Intensity (100%), and weld prep Side Gap (mm).
  • Corner Looping (Sharp Direction Changes): Set the minimum/maximum corner angle range (90°–180°) and loop radius (3 mm) used when injecting rollout corner loops into scrap to keep sharp external corners crisp.
  • Manage & Persist Profiles: Use the bottom-left toolbar (+ New, Clone, ★ Active) to create or switch profiles, then click the green Save Profile button in the top-right corner of the modal.
