Why Accurate Machine Configuration Matters

Unlike flat-sheet 2D plasma tables, a 4-axis rotary tube cutter simultaneously coordinates linear axes (X, Y, Z) in millimeters with a rotary chuck axis (A) in degrees. When sweeping around rectangular HSS tube corners or cutting deep pipe saddles, the rotary chuck must rapidly accelerate and decelerate while keeping the plasma arc's surface velocity constant. RotaryCAM uses your table's exact physical motor limits to calculate smooth rotary acceleration, keep chuck motors from jerking or stalling on sharp tube corners, and freeze your Torch Height Control (THC) before the torch slows down.

Step 02

Configure Accurate Axis Kinematics, Velocity, Acceleration & Safe Z Limits

The first tab, Axis Kinematics & Limits, defines the physical capabilities of your CNC table's linear motors (X, Y, Z) and rotary chuck drive (A). Entering your table's real limits ensures RotaryCAM matches your machine's true motor speeds and never asks your chuck to spin faster than it physically can.

  • Rotary Drive Ratio & Direction: Specify your chuck reduction ratio (e.g., 10:1 Rotary) and whether +A rotation follows right-hand or inverted conventions.
  • Linear Axes (X / Y / Z) Limits: Set the true maximum rapid velocity (mm/min), maximum acceleration (mm/s²), and S-curve jerk (mm/s³) your drives can sustain without losing steps or faulting.
  • Rotary Chuck (A-Axis) Limits: Set the maximum rotational speed (deg/min, e.g., 36000°/min = 100 RPM), angular acceleration (deg/s²), and angular jerk (deg/s³). During rectangular tube corner transitions, chuck rotation is almost always the physical bottleneck. RotaryCAM uses these limits to smooth out corner speeds so your steppers or servos never lose steps.
  • Safe Z Clearance Height: Defines the safe retract envelope (e.g., 25 mm) above the rotating tube's maximum diagonal radius so rapid moves never clip a spinning square/rectangular corner.
  • Chuck Jaw Dead Zone (65 mm), Min Chuck Grip (80 mm) & Roller “No-Cut” Zones: Enter your chuck jaw keep-out distance (chuckJawDeadZoneMm, default 65 mm), minimum chuck clamping grip length (minChuckGripLengthMm, default 80 mm), and any steady-rest or roller keep-out bands. RotaryCAM displays a translucent red no-cut zone and 3D chuck jaws in the CAD viewer and 3D simulator, subtracts the dead zone from raw stick capacity during 1D tube nesting, shows the exact Min Stock to Load (L_cut + L_dead), and warns you before G-code runs if a cut reaches into the chuck jaws.

NEW IN v0.7.1 Coordinate System, Axis Orientation & Motor Polarity

Plasma tables come in different shop layouts. Some rotary chucks sit along the long X rail; others mount parallel to the Y gantry rail. Flat sheet jobs might be loaded with the long edge along X or Y, zeroed at any corner or plate center. RotaryCAM gives you full native control over rotary alignment, 4 travel modes, 6 flat sheet work origins, and motor DIR polarity so G-code runs true on your table without manual coordinate edits or flipping motor wiring.

1. Rotary Tube Axis: Along X (Default) vs. Along Y (Gantry-Parallel)

Select which linear axis runs down the length of your tube:

  • Tube Along X (Standard Rotary Table): The rotary chuck spins around the machine's X-axis. Tube length runs along X, lateral cross-torch travel moves on Y, and rotary rotation spins A. This is the default setup on standalone pipe cutters and front-mounted rotary attachments.
  • Tube Along Y (Gantry-Parallel / Outboard Rotary Trough): For dual-purpose 4x8 or 5x10 tables (such as FlashCut CNC, ShopSabre, or custom water tables with an outboard pipe trough) where the rotary attachment sits along the long Y rail. Selecting Tube Along Y applies a true right-handed +90° coordinate rotation (L → Y, T → -X). Longitudinal cuts move on Y, cross-torch lateral shifts (such as off-center HSS corner climbs or bevel passes) move on X, and velocity/accel limits automatically pull from the physical axis moving the torch. Because this is a true 3D spatial rotation rather than a coordinate mirror, asymmetric fishmouths, angled saddle branches, and hole offsets are never mirrored.
  • 3-Axis Linear Wrap (RotaryAxisMapping): For controllers without a native 4th rotary axis (such as Langmuir FireControl or 3-axis Mach3), RotaryCAM automatically wraps circumference onto physical linear travel. When the tube is along X, circumference wraps onto Y (G92 Y0). When the tube is along Y, circumference wraps onto X (G92 X0).

3. Flat Sheet Orientation (0° vs. 90° CCW) & 6 Work Zero Origins

When cutting 2D plate parts from DXF or SVG files, align parts with your table slats and choose where the operator zeroes the torch:

  • Sheet Orientation (0° vs. 90° CCW): Select 0° (Part Length along +X) to lay long parts along the X rail, or 90° CCW (Part Length along +Y) to orient parts along the Y gantry rail. This is a rigid Cartesian rotation (X' = -Y, Y' = X), guaranteeing asymmetric brackets, offset holes, and engraved text markings are never mirrored.
  • 6 Work Zero (X0, Y0) Origins:
    • Bottom-Left ([0, +X] x [0, +Y]): Industry default on standard 2D plasma tables where the operator stands at the front-left corner.
    • Top-Left ([0, +X] x [-Y, 0]): Zeroes at the rear-left corner with forward-moving Y travel.
    • Bottom-Right ([-X, 0] x [0, +Y]): Zeroes at the front-right corner for right-hand loading tables.
    • Top-Right ([-X, 0] x [-Y, 0]): Zeroes at the rear-right corner with negative X and Y travel.
    • Center ([-X/2, +X/2] x [-Y/2, +Y/2]): Places X0, Y0 dead center of the sheet bounding box. Ideal for cutting small drops, circular flanges, or nesting inside remnant plates zeroed with a center punch mark.
    • Moving Sheet (-X → 0): For automated pinch-roller or plate-feeder machines that push steel forward beneath a stationary cutting bridge.

4. Hardware Motor Polarity (Invert X, Invert Y, Invert Rotary A)

💡 The Polarity Rule of Thumb

Always set your Fixture & Datum (Tube Axis, Travel Mode, and Sheet Origin) first so the 3D viewport matches how raw stock physically sits on your slats or chuck. Only toggle Invert X, Invert Y, or Invert Rotary A if a motor's DIR wire is reversed or your chuck rotates backward.

When an axis inversion checkbox is enabled, RotaryCAM does not simply negate coordinate values in post. It handles the complete mathematical transformation:

  • Negates the physical axis coordinates in all G0 rapid and G1 cutting blocks.
  • Inverts directional tool offsets, including G52 pneumatic air-scribe and center-punch fixture offsets.
  • Automatically flips circular arc direction (G2 clockwise ↔ G3 counter-clockwise) across active planes (G17, G18, G19). This ensures lead-in arcs and kerf offsets stay on the scrap side and never gouge into finished part walls.
✓ Multiple Profiles Persisted Across Restarts

You can maintain separate profiles for different machines, rotary chucks, or plasma power sources using the left list (+ New or Clone). All configured profiles are automatically saved and persisted across browser/app restarts.

⚠ Critical: Why G93 Inverse-Time Is Mandatory for 4-Axis Rotary Cutting

On a flat plate or fixed-radius round tube, a standard units-per-minute feedrate (G94 F3000) works fine. But when cutting square/rectangular HSS tubing, angle iron, or pipe bevels, the distance from the tube's center to the cutting point changes continuously as the chuck rotates. Standard controller "Rotary Radius Compensation" settings only work for a single fixed circular radius and fail on rectangular corners. This causes the machine to violently surge or crawl. In G93 Inverse-Time mode, RotaryCAM calculates the exact physical time (Δt) required for each micro-segment and writes F = 1 / Δt on every line, guaranteeing your plasma torch cuts at the exact surface speed you set in your cut chart, regardless of stock shape or corner radius.

Beneath the dialect selector, configure your Post-Processor Compensation Strategy:

  • Enable Acceleration & Jerk Profiling: Models your machine's real motor acceleration in software so feedrate changes and THC anti-dive freeze points match what happens on the table.
  • Smooth Rotary Corner Motion (Square & Rectangular Tube): Automatically smooths chuck rotation when rolling from flat faces into corner fillets, preventing sudden rotational jerks and motor stalls.
  • Enable Kerf Width Compensation & Kerf-True Tabs: Offsets inside holes (CW) and outside cuts (CCW) by half your plasma kerf width so parts hit dimension right off the table, and adds a full kerf width to micro-joint tab gaps so thin-wall tabs never burn away.
  • Rotary Small-Hole (60%) Bolt Speed Scaling: Automatically slows enclosed bolt holes (≤ 25.4 mm / 1″) on round pipe and square/rectangular tubing to 60% cutting speed, stopping plasma arc-lag cone taper so bolts drop straight through without reaming.
  • Configurable ERW Weld-Seam Pierce Avoidance: Set where your tube's internal ERW mill weld seam faces in the chuck on round pipe or square/rectangular HSS, and pierces automatically shift onto clean parent steel, preventing blowback spatter from thick internal weld flash.
  • Heat-Optimized vs. One-Direction Cut Ordering: Choose between automatic heat staggering (alternating internal holes and slots along the tube to reduce heat bow) or one-direction cutting from the free end toward the chuck.
Step 04

Configure Synchronous THC Lock/Unlock & Plasma Hardware

Select the THC & Plasma Hardware tab. On a 4-axis tube cutter, Torch Height Control (THC) configuration is one of the most important settings in the entire CAM pipeline.

When the torch rolls around a rectangular tube corner or a steep saddle apex, the rotary chuck must slow down to stay within its angular acceleration limit. Whenever plasma cutting speed drops, the plasma arc column widens and arc voltage rises sharply. An unlocked voltage-based THC misinterprets that voltage spike as "torch too high" and drives the torch straight into the steel tube.

  • Plasma Torch / Cut Chart Family: Select your power supply's torch family once on each machine profile:
    • Hypertherm SYNC (3.2 mm / 0.125" cut height): Powermax 45/65/85/105 SYNC single-piece cartridge platform (810500MU Rev 4, 45A–105A).
    • Hypertherm Duramax / Legacy 30XP–105 (1.5 mm / 0.060" cut height): Powermax 30XP through 105 torches running multi-piece mechanized shielded consumables (30A–105A + 45A FineCut).
    • Generic Air Plasma (1.5 mm / 0.060" cut height): Conservative blowback torches (30A, 45A, 60A, 80A, ~106–128 V, 72 PSI) for Razorweld 45, PrimeWeld Cut60, Everlast PowerPlasma, and Thermal Dynamics Cutmaster.
    Setting your torch family binds the correct standoff height (1.5 mm vs 3.2 mm), arc voltage, and pierce delay automatically whenever a 3D tube or flat sheet is loaded.
  • THC Mode & Lock Command Syntax: Choose how RotaryCAM signals your THC to freeze and unfreeze in G-code, such as synchronous digital output macros (M63 P0 to lock / M62 P0 to unlock without motion planner pauses) or controller-specific M-codes.
  • Dynamic Corner Lock Threshold (%): Automatically locks Z-axis height regulation whenever actual surface velocity drops below the configured percentage (e.g., 80%) of target cutting speed, and unlocks it once full surface speed resumes.
  • Lead-In / Lead-Out THC Lock: Keeps THC locked during pierce, ramp lead-in, and overburn lead-out phases where the plasma arc is stabilizing or crossing an existing kerf.
⚡ Why Synchronous M62/M63 Matters

Standard M64/M65 or custom M-codes on many controllers force an exact-stop motion buffer flush, causing the torch to pause momentarily and blow a divot in the tube wall. Synchronous M62/M63 commands toggle the THC lock pin in lockstep with the next G1 motion block at full lookahead speed.

Step 05

Set Default Zero-Bevel & Corner Loop Parameters and Save Profile

Finally, open the Zero-Bevel & Corner Loops tab to define the default geometric compensation values applied to new contours (which can still be overridden per contour during part inspection):

  • Zero-Bevel Pipe Saddle & Square/Rect HSS Miter Defaults: Set default Fade Angle (90°), Intensity (100%), and weld prep Side Gap (mm) for dual-curve round pipe saddles and heel-only pullback (t · tan(θ)) on square/rectangular HSS miters, plus default ERW weld-seam exclusion zones so pierces stay off internal mill flash.
  • Corner Looping (Sharp Direction Changes): Set the minimum/maximum corner angle range (90°–180°) and loop radius (3 mm) used when injecting rollout corner loops into scrap to keep sharp external corners crisp.
  • Manage & Persist Profiles: Use the bottom-left toolbar (+ New, Clone, ★ Active) to create or switch profiles, then click the green Save Profile button in the top-right corner of the modal.

Step 07

Controller XML Import Warnings FAQ

When you import a controller XML file into RotaryCAM, the importer flags settings that can stall your rotary chuck, mess up pierce delays, or scale parts wrong. Find your exact warning below to see what it means on your table and what to fix before cutting.

ArtSoft Mach3 • Profile .xml Warnings • Mach3 Page →

1. ArtSoft Mach3 (.xml) Import Warnings

Unrecognized or Missing <Units>

What it means: Your Mach3 XML did not specify inches or millimeters, so RotaryCAM defaulted Machine Units to mm. If your steps/unit in Motor Tuning are calibrated in inches, parts will cut 25.4x the wrong size.
What to do: Check Machine Units (mm vs. in) in the RotaryCAM Machines modal and match your Mach3 Motor Tuning units.

Motor 3 (A-Axis) Slaved to X / Y / Z (Slave0=0/1/2)

What it means: Your table uses Motor 3 (A-axis) as the second motor on a dual-drive gantry (common on Bulltear Plasma and HVAC gantry tables) instead of a rotary chuck.
What to do: Flat-sheet DXF/SVG cuts work right away with zero changes. To cut round pipe on a dual-drive table, either switch Rotary Axis Mapping to 3-Axis Y-Axis Wrap (and plug your rotary into Y), or unslave Motor 3 in Mach3 (Config → Slave Axis) if you move dual-drive slaving to hardware or the B-axis.

A-Axis Is Set to Linear Mode (<AAngular>=0)

What it means: Mach3 treats your A-axis as linear millimeters or inches instead of degrees, so G93 rotary speeds and chuck rotations will be way off.
What to do: In Mach3, open Config → General Config, check A-Axis is Angular, save, and re-import your XML (or type your true A-axis deg/min limits directly into RotaryCAM).

4th Axis Disabled (<AAxisOn>=0)

What it means: The A-axis output is turned off in your Mach3 profile.
What to do: Flat-sheet cuts work as-is with no changes. For 4-axis pipe cutting, enable Axis A in Mach3 (Config → Ports & Pins → Motor Outputs), or switch RotaryCAM to 3-Axis Y-Axis Wrap.

Spindle Spin-Up / Spin-Down Delay Active (SpinDelayCW > 0)

What it means: Mach3 adds an extra hidden pause after every M3 torch fire on top of RotaryCAM's G04 pierce delay, causing blown pierces and arc-loss faults.
What to do: Open Config → Ports & Pins → Spindle Setup in Mach3 and set CW and CCW Delay Spin UP and Spin DOWN to 0.

Mill / Spindle Screenset Detected

What it means: Your Mach3 profile loads a router or mill screenset (.set) instead of a plasma screenset, so on-screen THC controls and Arc OK LEDs may be missing.
What to do: Load a plasma screenset in Mach3 (View → Load Screens), or verify your M3/M5 torch output and THC pins before cutting.

G04 Dwell in Milliseconds (<DwellinMilli>=1)

What it means: Mach3 reads G04 P0.5 as 0.5 milliseconds instead of 0.5 seconds, so your torch plunges before piercing through the plate.
What to do: Open Config → General Config in Mach3 and uncheck G04 Dwell in ms so Mach3 reads RotaryCAM's G04 P dwells in seconds.

Plasma Mode or CV Feedrate Active (<PlasmaMode>=1 / <CVFeedOn>=1)

What it means: Mach3's built-in Plasma Mode, CV Dist Tolerance, and CV Feedrate fight G93 inverse-time rotary blending and cause choppy chuck motion.
What to do: In Mach3 Config → General Config, uncheck Plasma Mode and CV Dist Tolerance, then open the Settings (Alt-6) tab and turn CV Feedrate OFF.

LookAhead Too High (<LookAhead> > 200)

What it means: LookAhead values above 200 lines in Mach3 can overflow the motion planner buffer and stutter on dense 4-axis tube saddle toolpaths.
What to do: Open Config → General Config in Mach3 and set LookAhead to 200 lines.

Absolute IJ Arc Mode (<IJMode>=0)

What it means: Your Mach3 default is Absolute IJ instead of Incremental IJ. RotaryCAM emits G91.1 at the top of every file to force Incremental IJ, so normal runs cut fine, but mid-program restarts can drop back to Absolute IJ and throw crop-circle arcs.
What to do: Switch IJ Mode to Inc in Mach3 Config → General Config so mid-program Run From Here restarts stay safe.

Safe Z Height Zero or Negative (m_SafeZ <= 0)

What it means: Mach3 has no positive Safe Z retract height configured, so pausing or stopping mid-cut will not clear clamps or rotating tube corners.
What to do: Set a positive Safe Z height in Mach3 (Config → Safe_Z Setup) and verify your Safe Z Clearance in RotaryCAM clears your biggest tube diagonal.

Warp9 ESS + G28.1 Probing Warning

What it means: Selecting G28.1 touch-off probing with a Warp9 Ethernet SmoothStepper (ESS) often hangs the controller during floating-head probing.
What to do: Keep RotaryCAM's Touch-Off Strategy on G31 Probe (the Mach3 default) instead of G28.1.

FlashCut CNC • Setup.xml Warnings • FlashCut Page →

2. FlashCut CNC (Setup.xml) Import Warnings

Unrecognized or Missing SystemUnits

What it means: RotaryCAM could not read SystemUnits from your FlashCut Setup.xml and defaulted Machine Units to mm.
What to do: Check Machine Units (mm vs. in) in the RotaryCAM Machines modal and match your FlashCut table units.

Missing or Unexpected AxisParallel

What it means: Your Setup.xml did not specify which gantry rail your rotary attachment runs along, so RotaryCAM defaulted Rotary Tube Axis to X.
What to do: If your rotary chuck sits parallel to the Y gantry rail, select Parallel to Y-Axis in RotaryCAM's Machines modal.

Plasma Head ID Not Found in <Machine><FabHeads>

What it means: RotaryCAM could not match your requested Plasma Head ID or found no *PlasmaHead entries in your FlashCut XML, so head ID, arc transfer, and torch M-codes could not be auto-filled.
What to do: Pick your active plasma head from the dropdown in RotaryCAM, or type your Head ID (e.g. Hplasma1), Arc Transfer Input, and M50/M51 M-codes manually.

ZeroLiftAxisPositionAfterTouchOff="False"

What it means: FlashCut is not resetting its Z lift coordinate to zero after G605 touch-off probing. Standard 2D flat-sheet and round-pipe cuts work as-is, but 3D Ramp Lead-Ins and 4D square/angle tubes need a zeroed Z datum.
What to do: Enable Zero Lift Axis Position After Touch Off inside FlashCut (Setup → FabHeads) before running 3D ramps or 4D structural tubing.

Missing M101 I<n> Arc Transfer Input

What it means: RotaryCAM could not find M101 I<n> in your Cut2D start macro (or had to guess from InputLines), so it cannot confirm which input waits for Arc OK.
What to do: Open FlashCut (Setup → I/O → Inputs), find your Arc Transfer / Arc OK input line number, and enter that number in RotaryCAM's FlashCut settings.

CSLab SimCNC 3.6 • config.xml Warnings • SimCNC Page →

3. CSLab SimCNC 3.6 (config.xml) Import Warnings

Unrecognized or Missing <baseUnits>

What it means: Your SimCNC config.xml did not list <baseUnits>, so RotaryCAM defaulted Machine Units to mm.
What to do: Check Machine Units (mm vs. in) in RotaryCAM and confirm it matches your SimCNC Configuration units.

axis_3 (A-Axis) Disabled, Unbound, or Set to linear

What it means: In SimCNC, axis_3 (A) is turned off, has no MotionKit assigned, or is configured as a linear axis instead of angular.
What to do: Flat-sheet cuts work as-is. For 4-axis pipe cutting, open SimCNC (Configuration → Axes), enable axis_3, set its type to angular, bind a MotionKit, and re-import config.xml.

Missing <motionKit_N> or Mismatched Dual-Drive Gantry Limits

What it means: Either a <motionKit_N> block is missing under <device><modules>, or the two motors on a dual-drive gantry have different velocity, acceleration, or jerk limits (which can rack the gantry).
What to do: Copy Max Velocity, Accel, and Jerk from SimCNC (Configuration → Modules) into RotaryCAM manually, and make sure both dual-drive gantry MotionKits use identical limits in SimCNC.

<ijkMode> Not Set to relative

What it means: SimCNC is expecting Absolute IJK arc centers while RotaryCAM outputs Incremental (Relative) IJK arcs (and does not emit G90.1/G91.1 on SimCNC), causing huge unwanted arc loops or soft-limit errors.
What to do: Open SimCNC (Configuration → General) and set Arc IJK Mode to Relative.
