06. Fusion 360 & AI Profile Autodetection

Model Tubes in Fusion 360 Structural Member

When you drop a STEP file, your tubes are scanned automatically for cross-section family (Profile_HSS_Rect, Profile_HSS_Round, Profile_Angle, Profile_Channel), outer dimensions, and wall thickness.

⚠️
Use Autodesk Fusion 360 + Structural Member: Every CAD program writes slightly different surface math. Right now, automatic tube scanning is calibrated for Autodesk Fusion 360 assemblies built with the Structural Member (SM) tool. Follow these 8 steps so your parts size and nest cleanly on the first load.
CAD Environment Autodesk Fusion 360 + SM Add-In
Bend Unfolding Prep Modify → Combine Connected Tube Bodies
Automatic Setup Auto Profile & Pack Grouping
Step 01

Open Autodesk Fusion 360 & Verify the Structural Member Add-In

Launch Autodesk Fusion 360 and open a new design (such as rotarycam-demo):

  • 1. Check the Structural Member Icons: In DESIGN → SOLID → CREATE, verify the four blue Structural Member icons (SM Structural Member, Miter, Notch, and Trim/Extend) are present.
  • 2. Set Document Units: Under Document Settings in the left Browser, work in millimeters (mm, g) or inches. You can still pick either imperial or metric tube sizes inside the Structural Member library.
Step 02

Click Create Sketch & Select a Reference Plane

Pick a plane for your tube centerline sketch:

  • 1. Click Create Sketch: Click Create Sketch at the left end of SOLID → CREATE.
  • 2. Pick an Origin Plane: Click XY, XZ, or YZ in the 3D viewport.
Step 03

Draw Your Tube Centerline Sketch & Click Finish Sketch

Draw the centerline wireframe for your frame or bent tube:

  • 1. Draw Straight Runs & Bend Arcs: Use Line (L) for straight tubes and Fillet (F) or tangent arcs for bend corners. Here, the left vertical line is a straight square HSS post, and the D-shaped loop with two corner radii is a bent tube meeting that post.
  • 2. Click ✓ Finish Sketch: Click Finish Sketch in the top-right toolbar (or Sketch Palette) to return to the 3D Solid workspace.
Step 04

Use the Structural Member Plugin to Populate 3D Tube Profiles

Turn your centerline sketch into hollow HSS or round pipe solids and cope the joints:

  • 1. Open Structural Member (4a): Click CREATE → Structural Member → Structural Member Command (or press the SM ribbon icon). The same menu gives you Miter Command, Notch Command, and Trim Extend Command for coping joints.
  • 2. Pick Lines & Profile Size (4b): Click a sketch segment, then set your tube in STRUCTURAL MEMBER COMMAND. For this post: Standard: ANSI_AISC, Family: HSS, Size: 1-1/2 x 1-1/2 (38.1 × 38.1 mm), and Thickness: 1/8 (3.17 mm). Click OK.
  • 3. Sweep & Notch the Remaining Loop (4c): Repeat for the other segments using round pipe around the U-shaped loop (SM0001:1 through SM0007:1 in the Browser). Run Notch / Trim Extend so the round loop saddles cleanly onto the square post.
Step 05

Combine Bent Tube Segments (Only for Cut-and-Bend)

Structural Member creates a separate body for every line and arc in your sketch (SM0002:1 through SM0007:1).

  • Cutting straight separate tubes? Leave each structural member as its own body.
  • Unfolding a bent tube for Cut-and-Bend? Click Modify → Combine. Pick the first bent segment as Target Body, then select all connected elbows and straight legs of that tube as Tool Bodies. Set Operation to Join, uncheck New Component and Keep Tools, and click OK.
💡
Why Combine for Bending: Joining the straight legs and elbows into one solid body tells the bend unfolder that those pieces come from one continuous stick of stock with kerf-bending relief cuts (see Tutorial 04: Bending HSS & HSS-O). Do not combine separate tubes that weld together at a joint. Keep the vertical square post SM0001:1 separate from the curved round loop.
Step 06

Export a STEP File (.step / .stp) of the Entire Design

Export the whole assembly in one STEP file:

  • 1. Open File → Export: Click File in the top-left bar and choose Export...
  • 2. Pick STEP Format: Set Type to STEP Files (*.stp *.step).
  • 3. Click Export: Save your .step file.
Step 07

Load the Exported STEP File into RotaryCAM

Open RotaryCAM (Pipe CAM) and load your rotarycam-demo.step file:

  • 1. Drop Your STEP File (7a): Drag rotarycam-demo.step into CAD INGESTION (Drop STEP or ZIP Bundle) in the left sidebar, or click to browse.
  • 2. Click Load & Process CAD (7b): Preview your 3D assembly in the viewport. Keep ☑ One-Shot checked in the top bar, then click Load & Process CAD to extract features, unfold bends, and build G-code automatically.
Step 08

Check Autodetected Tube Profiles & Pack Groups

Once processing finishes, the built-in tube scanner identifies every profile in your assembly with zero manual typing:

  • 1. Check Cross-Section & Dimensions (AI Badge): Look at CROSS-SECTION & DIMENSIONS in the left sidebar. You will see Profile_HSS_Rect, outer dimensions 38.1×38.1 mm (1-1/2″ × 1-1/2″), wall thickness 3.17 mm (1/8″), and stock length 110.0 mm.
  • 2. Check Pack Nesting Groups: Open the CUTTING PROFILE dropdown in the center bar. Both tube profiles (Profile_HSS_Rect (38.1x38.1mm) (1 part) and Profile_HSS_Round (OD 42.2mm) (1 part)) are grouped separately by stock size, ready to simulate and export G-code.
