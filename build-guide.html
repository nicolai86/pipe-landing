Section 01

Machine Envelope & Structural Architecture

Designed for production fabrication shops cutting raw 20-foot (6-meter) structural mill sticks without pre-cutting on a cold saw.

STRUCTURAL FOUNDATION

5" × 3" & 4" × 4" Structural Steel Frame

The machine bed is built upon a single, continuous 6-meter 5.0" × 3.0" × 1/4" (127 × 76 × 6.35 mm) rectangular HSS structural tube. This main structural backbone provides immense torsional rigidity, preventing bed sag under heavy thick-wall pipe and resisting high dynamic acceleration forces from rapid gantry reversals.

The front slewing ring headstock and rear chuck tailstock frames are fabricated from a 20-foot stick of 4.0" × 4.0" × 1/4" square HSS, providing rigid structural framing to handle high rotary torque without deflection.

Heavy-duty adjustable leveling pedestals spaced along the spine allow precision laser-level alignment across the entire 20-foot bed length, isolating the machine from uneven shop floors.

KINEMATICS OVERVIEW

True 4-Axis Synchronized Motion

Unlike 2-axis rotary attachments that disconnect a flat-table Y-axis, this machine features true dedicated 4-axis simultaneous kinematics:

  • X-Axis (Longitudinal): 6,096 mm (20 ft) travel along the bed length for cutting mill sticks.
  • Y-Axis (Transverse): 20" (508 mm) cross-slide carriage for cutting across wide pipe profiles, slots, and off-center holes.
  • Z-Axis (Vertical Lifter): Fast initial height sensing (IHS) retracts and active THC arc voltage tracking.
  • A-Axis (Rotary): Continuous 360° stock rotation driven by dual synchronized chucks.

ROTARYCAM 4D FEEDRATE

The Rotary Feedrate Problem & CAM Solution

Standard CNC controllers (including SimCNC) calculate feedrate (F) in linear units-per-minute (inches/min or mm/min) or pure rotary degrees-per-minute. They cannot natively calculate constant surface speed when blending linear X/Y moves with rotary A-axis rotation.

If you cut an X-slot at F100, the torch travels at 100 IPM. But when cutting around a 2-inch pipe circumference, pure rotary motion at the same F drops or spikes surface speed, causing severe dross or arc extinguish. On square tube, the surface radius constantly changes between flat faces and corners!

How RotaryCAM Solves This (G94 & G93 Controllers)

If your controller only supports standard G94 (units-per-minute): You can still use RotaryCAM without any hardware changes. RotaryCAM pre-computes 4D Euclidean trajectory feedrate scaling directly into the G-code, recalculating feedrates dynamically for every motion segment to maintain constant surface torch velocity across flat tube faces and corner radii.

If your controller supports G93 (inverse time feedrate): You're all set also. RotaryCAM natively outputs standard G93 inverse time mode (supported on Mach4, LinuxCNC, Masso, and modern CNCs), where every rotary block specifies the exact reciprocal time required for constant surface speed cutting.

HARDWARE SYNCHRONIZATION • RS422 SPLITTER V1

Active RS422 Differential Step/Dir Splitter

The Architecture Challenge: This machine is controlled by a cost-effective 4-axis industrial motion controller (CSLab CSMIO/IP-M: X, Y, Z, A). However, to rotate a full 20-foot (6-meter) structural tube without torsional twist or angle lag between ends, the rotary axis is driven by two synchronized NEMA 34 Teknic ClearPath servos—one at the rear ZCT200 rotary actuator and one at the front XSU080318 slewing ring headstock.

Because the controller only has one physical A-axis step/dir channel, both servos must receive the exact same pulse train. Wiring two high-speed differential servo inputs in raw parallel to a single controller channel causes transmission line impedance mismatches, signal reflections, and pulse jitter at 125 kHz.

The Hardware Solution: I designed an active differential RS422 splitter PCB. It receives the single differential Step/Dir pulse stream from the IP-M, buffers the signals through high-speed RS-422 line receivers, and drives two independent, impedance-matched, actively buffered differential output pairs. Both servos receive zero-latency pulses in perfect hardware lockstep with zero software overhead.

AUTONOMOUS DSP • HARDWARE V1

Real-Time 10 kHz Step/Dir Interposer THC

Most CNC plasma height controllers rely on sluggish UP/DOWN bang-bang relays or closed proprietary lifter drives. This custom controller is a standalone 10 kHz RS-422 differential Step/Dir interposer powered by a 170 MHz ARM Cortex-M4F DSP that sits directly inline between the CSLab IP-M motion controller and the Z-axis ClearPath servo.

During rapids, initial height sensing (IHS) touch-offs, and pierce lifts, it acts as a zero-drift digital pass-through. The instant the plasma arc transfers and THC_ENA asserts, the DSP blends real-time arc-voltage corrections directly into the step stream.

What This Means for Cut Quality & Process Reliability

±0.25V (~120 μm) Standoff Regulation: Holding torch height within fractions of a millimeter across a 20-foot pipe completely eliminates bevel angle variation, producing square, perpendicular kerf walls ready to weld without angle-grinder fitting.

Dross & Slag Elimination: Prevents torch climbing (which flares the arc and leaves heavy bottom dross) and torch diving (which causes nozzle double-arcing and slag spatter contamination).

Predictive dV/dt Anti-Dive: Crossing pre-pierced holes or tube voids causes arc voltage to surge. The DSP detects dV/dt spikes within milliseconds and instantaneously freezes Z motion before the torch can dive into the void.

Zero-Drift Automatic Unwinding: Tracks all injected THC microsteps and smoothly unwinds back to the CNC's true commanded coordinate when the cut ends, guaranteeing zero accumulated Z drift across thousands of cuts.

01. Three Operating Modes: HYBRID_SAV, PURE_SAV & PRESET

Fixed book-voltage THCs (PRESET) fail as soon as an electrode wears (raising arc voltage by +5 V to +10 V and driving the torch into the plate) or when cutting far from the ground clamp. Conversely, traditional Sample-Arc-Voltage (PURE_SAV) systems solve consumable wear by locking onto the initial post-pierce voltage, but fail if warped thin sheet springs back after touch-off—locking in a bogus 165–170 V target (0.50"+ standoff) for the rest of the cut.

The firmware supports all three operating modes, with HYBRID_SAV as the default:

MODE 1 • PRODUCTION DEFAULT Dual ADC Architecture

1. HYBRID_SAV (Default — EWMV Plateau Lock + Cut-Chart Sanity Window)

Reads the live plasma arc on ADC and the CNC's cut-chart preset voltage on ADC2 (output by simCNC / m3.py). Driven by the statistical EWMV plateau engine, it combines the speed of sample-arc-voltage with the safety of cut-chart bounds:

Statistical EWMV Plateau Lock

12–78 ms (41.5 ms mean): Instead of waiting out a fixed 150–500 ms delay timer after pierce, HYBRID_SAV computes the real-time Exponentially Weighted Moving Variance (EWMV) of the arc voltage at 10 kHz. As soon as the plasma column stabilizes into a laminar cut (σ² ≤ threshold for 10.0 ms), it locks the target voltage in as little as 12 ms—engaging active tracking within 0.13" (3.3 mm) of reaching full XY cut speed.

Asymmetric Cut-Chart Clamping

[-7.0 V, +15.0 V]: If the natural plateau sits inside [Preset - 7.0 V, Preset + 15.0 V], it locks the true measured voltage (automatically compensating for consumable wear, work-lead drop, and FineCut arc drag). If warped metal causes the pierce to start out of bounds (e.g., 165 V), it clamps the target to the window boundary and immediately drives the torch back to proper cut height (THC_STATE_TRACKING), never freezing the axis.

Automatic PURE_SAV Fallback

ADC2 < 55 V Auto-Detect: If no valid ADC2 preset is present (ADC2 < 55 V, e.g., after an E-Stop reset or on a CNC controller without an analog output), HYBRID_SAV automatically falls back to PURE_SAV ([55 V .. 200 V] global bounds) with zero operator intervention.

MODE 2 • AUTONOMOUS RETROFIT

2. PURE_SAV (Autonomous EWMV Plateau Lock)

Uses the exact same 10 kHz EWMV statistical plateau detector to lock the post-pierce arc voltage in < 45 ms mean, ignoring ADC2 completely and bounding the target only to the global [55 V .. 200 V] cutting range. Ideal for standalone retrofits without an analog target output from the CNC.

MODE 3 • DIRECT CNC TRACKING

3. PRESET (Direct Cut-Chart Tracking)

Bypasses THC_STATE_SAMPLING and tracks directly to the live ADC2 analog setpoint from the CNC controller. Useful for dialed-in book-spec cuts with fresh consumables where strict cut-chart voltage compliance is required.

02. Deterministic DSP Control Loop Innovations

Running at 10 kHz (100 µs cycle time) on a hardware interrupt timer, the firmware integrates four mathematical and physical filters designed specifically for rotary plasma kinematics:

SIGNAL CONDITIONING

4th-Order Bessel Low-Pass Filter (1 kHz)

Sampled at Fs = 10 kHz with fc = 1 kHz. Maximally flat group delay (0.33 ms) strips 20–100 kHz plasma inverter switching hash while preserving true arc-voltage transients without phase distortion or ringing.

SERVO ADAPTATION

Asymmetric Adaptive Gain & Upward Fast-Bleed Integrator

Inside fine regulation (|error| ≤ 1.5 V), baseline gain of 3.06 mm/s/V keeps the torch dead-calm on flat plate. On steep upward ramps (|error| → 6.0 V), gain scales smoothly to 6.80 mm/s/V alongside an upward-only leaky velocity integrator (thc_slope_integrator ≤ 0).

Eliminates uphill lag (< 0.4 V steady-state lag on a 1.12" ramp) and fast-drains in < 3 ms (τ = 0.64 ms) at crest turnarounds. Downward tracking uses pure proportional control with zero downward integral windup, preventing dives into warped sheet or voids.

COLLISION PREVENTION

Motion-Compensated Kerf Anti-Dive (dV/dt Observer)

Evaluates external voltage rate-of-change over a 3.0 ms (30-tick) rolling window (averaging across 0.2–0.8 ms plasma anodic-restrike sawtooth cycles) while subtracting the voltage rise caused by the torch's own upward step emissions (v_motion_q4). Crossing a prior kerf freezes downward motion in 100 µs and arrests downward S-curve momentum immediately while still permitting upward collision retraction.

POSITION INTEGRITY

3rd-Order S-Curve Profiler & Baseline Return

Physical velocity, acceleration, and jerk limits from machines.json are enforced in hardware on both THC_STATE_TRACKING and post-cut THC_STATE_RETURNING. Prevents stepper stalls while automatically unwinding accumulated THC steps at the end of every cut, guaranteeing the CNC controller DRO never drifts even across thousands of pierce cycles.

Frequently Asked Questions

Practical advice on motor selection, ohmic sensing options, bed scaling, and custom parts sourcing.

MOTORS & DRIVES

Do I have to use Teknic ClearPath servos, or will steppers work?

Short answer: No, but servos are highly recommended.

Closed-loop steppers (such as NEMA 34 closed-loop stepper kits) can work if budget is strictly limited. However, rotating a 20-foot (6-meter) steel tube introduces significant rotational inertia. At high rotary rapid speeds (up to 36,000°/min), standard steppers lose torque quickly and risk stalling on heavy wall pipe.

Teknic ClearPath servos maintain 100% constant peak torque across their entire RPM band. Crucially, with dual synchronized motors driving front and rear chucks simultaneously, true closed-loop servos ensure both chucks remain locked in electronic lockstep, preventing torsional tube twist on thin-wall tubing.

TORCH SENSING

Is the FastCutCNC ohmic box & Hypertherm interface mandatory?

Short answer: Not strictly mandatory, but it saved days of wiring and troubleshooting.

You can build your own relay-isolated ohmic sensing circuit or rely exclusively on a mechanical floating head microswitch. However, plasma arcs generate violent high-voltage electrical noise and ground potential shifts that can easily blow unshielded 5V/24V controller inputs.

The FastCutCNC ohmic box ↗ provides industrial-grade opto-isolation between the plasma arc and the CSLab IP-M controller, and plugs directly into the Hypertherm machine interface cable. It provides plug-and-play arc transfer, arc voltage division, and reliable touch-off without ground loop interference.

BED SIZING

Can I build this with a shorter bed length (e.g. 10ft / 3m instead of 6m)?

Yes, the design is 100% scalable along the longitudinal axis.

If your fabrication shop does not process full 20ft raw mill sticks, you can easily cut down the 5" × 3" × 1/4" rectangular HSS spine beam and shorten the HGH25 linear rails and helical rack to 10 feet (3 meters) or 12 feet.

All headstock mounts, chuck adapters, servo brackets, cross-slide carriages, and Z-axis torch lifters remain 100% identical regardless of bed length.
