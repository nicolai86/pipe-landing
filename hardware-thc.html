Your Z motor isn’t too slow. Your CNC controller’s motion buffer is in the way.

PC and Ethernet CNCs route arc voltage through a 15–40 ms motion buffer. By the time software reacts, your torch has already dived into a corner or slammed into bowed plate.

Auto-Voltage Modes

Stop Chasing Cut-Chart Volts: Pure SAV vs. Hybrid SAV

Arc voltage climbs all afternoon as your electrode pits. Instead of bumping voltage by +1V every twenty pierces, Smart Auto-Voltage (SAV) sets physical cut height with your Z-axis first, then locks onto the live arc.

01 // Pure SAV Zero-Config

Pure SAV: Zero-Config “Set Z and Forget It”

How it works: Your 24V ohmic touch-off finds Z = 0, lifts to pierce height, pierces, and drops to cut height (for example, 0.060"). Once the arc settles, the THC locks onto that live plateau voltage for the rest of the cut.

On the table: Dial in your Z steps-per-unit and ohmic offset once, and stop typing arc volts into CAM. Fresh consumables, worn tips, and small shop-air swings track automatically until the electrode is spent.

Cabinet wiring bonus: Needs zero 0-10V analog target wires and zero RS-485 links from your CNC. Built for Mach3/4, Centroid Acorn, MASSO, FlashCut, or LinuxCNC cabinets.

02 // Hybrid SAV Thin-Sheet Clamped

Hybrid SAV: Thin-Sheet Bow Protection

How it works: Runs the same wear-tracking sample after every pierce, guarded by a [-7 V, +15 V] safety window around your cut-chart target (over 0-10V on J2 Pin 5 or RS-485).

On the table: Thin sheet (22 ga to 11 ga) and tight nests pop upward from heat right on the pierce. Plain auto-voltage locks onto that bowed height and cuts too high. Hybrid SAV lets +1 V to +10 V wear float inside the window while clamping pierce spikes back to true cut height.

Shop-floor bonus: Run unattended sheet nests with confidence. Even if a slat finger springs up or a nest bows 0.150", the clamp pulls your torch right back onto the cut plane.

Quick Guide: When to Run Which Mode

3/16″ (10 ga) and thicker plate Pure SAV Plate stays flat during the pierce. Your calibrated ohmic touch-off lands right on cut height every time with zero voltage setup.

The Only Requirement for Pure SAV

Pure SAV trusts your physical pierce-to-cut drop to lock the arc voltage. Set your Z steps/unit and ohmic offset once with a 0.060" feeler gauge, and the board takes care of voltage from there.

Tier 1 // 4 Plugs ~15 Min • Pure SAV

Pure SAV + Warp Tracking

Four plugs get you running with full-stroke warp tracking and zero CAM voltage entry:

  • 01[POWER]: Wire 24V DC cabinet power to 24V, 0V, and [GND].
  • 02[INPUT Z] → [OUTPUT Z]: Unplug your Z Step/Dir lead and snap the board inline to your Z drive.
  • 03[ARC VIN]: Feed isolated 0-10V divided arc voltage from your 50:1 CPC port or external HV divider card.
  • 04[ARC OK]: Connect your plasma ARC OK signal, or run wire-free arc detection.

Common Cabinet & Plasma Wiring Questions

“Does it work with single-ended 5V Step/Dir or only differential?”

X through C inputs and outputs use noise-immune RS-422 differential Step/Dir (S+/S-, D+/D-). If your breakout board only sends single-ended 5V Step/Dir, drop a small 5V single-ended to RS-422 line driver inline ahead of the THC inputs.

“Does it plug into my plasma cutter safely?”

✓ Plug-and-Play 50:1 Divided Arc Hypertherm CPC, Razorweld, and Everlast CNC ports: wire the low-voltage divided signal straight into your isolation card.
⚡ Raw-Arc Cutters Mount an external isolated HV voltage divider at the plasma unit so raw 300V torch leads never enter your CNC cabinet.

“Won’t injecting Z steps throw off my CNC’s Z DRO?”

Never. Hardware counts every +Z/-Z step and unwinds 100% back to zero when ARC OK drops, keeping your Z DRO dead on.
